Climate Change and Its Effects

Climate change refers to long-term shifts in global temperatures and weather patterns. Human activities have been the main driver of climate change since the industrial revolution of the 1800s.

The burning of fossil fuels releases carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gases into the atmosphere. These gases trap heat from the Sun and raise the average temperature of Earth's surface.

Evidence of Climate Change

Global average temperatures have risen by approximately 1.1 degrees Celsius since the pre-industrial period. Scientists recorded the ten hottest years on record within the last two decades.

Polar ice sheets and mountain glaciers are melting at an accelerating rate. The Arctic sea ice extent has declined dramatically over the past forty years.

Sea levels are rising because melting ice adds water to the oceans and because warmer water expands. Coastal communities face increasing risks of flooding and erosion.

Extreme weather events have become more frequent and intense. Hurricanes, droughts, wildfires, and heavy rainfall events now occur with greater regularity around the world.

Consequences for Ecosystems

Rising temperatures disrupt ecosystems and threaten biodiversity. Many species are shifting their geographic ranges toward higher altitudes and latitudes to track suitable climatic conditions.

Coral reefs are particularly vulnerable to ocean warming and acidification. Bleaching events have destroyed large portions of major reef systems, including the Great Barrier Reef.

Changes in precipitation patterns affect freshwater availability. Some regions experience prolonged droughts while others suffer from severe flooding, creating water insecurity for millions of people.

Mitigation Strategies

Reducing greenhouse gas emissions is essential to limiting further warming. Governments and industries must transition rapidly from fossil fuels to renewable energy sources such as solar and wind power.

Energy efficiency improvements in buildings, transportation, and manufacturing can significantly reduce emissions. Electric vehicles replace combustion engines and lower carbon output in the transport sector.

Forests absorb carbon dioxide from the atmosphere. Protecting existing forests and planting new trees help remove greenhouse gases and restore biodiversity.

International cooperation is critical because climate change is a global challenge. The Paris Agreement committed nations to limiting warming to well below two degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels.
